## 2. A step-time mystery, investigated rather than shrugged off

Step time dropped from ~137s/step (before the stop) to ~47s/step (after
the resume) — a ~3x change with no code or config difference. Checked
directly rather than assumed:

- `VRAM after setup` was identical both times (2848.6 MiB) — ruled out a
  memory-footprint explanation.
- The system had rebooted at 08:56:09 that morning, between the stop and
  the resume (`systeminfo` boot time, checked directly).
- The previous session ended with 14 background monitoring tasks flagged
  by the harness as not cleanly torn down — each running a `tail -f` loop
  plus a periodic subprocess launch. If several were still alive as
  orphaned processes, they would have been silently competing with the
  training process for CPU/disk I/O the whole time; a reboot would clear
  all of them at once.

Follow-up question from the user: was the 10-minute heartbeat monitor
itself (still legitimately running, not orphaned) a meaningful
contributor? Checked directly rather than assumed either way:
`generate_live_status.py` imports only Python stdlib (`datetime, json, os,
statistics, sys`) — no `torch`, so each invocation is sub-second. A small,
real, periodic effect *was* found in the post-resume log (steps landing
roughly every 8-10 minutes show a +3-4s bump, e.g. step 261: 50.9s vs. a
~47s baseline) — real, but roughly 20-30x too small to explain the
~90s/step gap from before the reboot. Conclusion: the heartbeat is a
minor, now-quantified contributor to noise on the step it overlaps, not
the explanation for the larger slowdown; the reboot/thermal-state
explanation remains better supported, with the direct evidence gap (no
GPU clock reading captured *during* the slow period) stated honestly
rather than papered over.

Two findings surfaced during this exercise that had not been written up
before, because no prior document had put all three models' numbers in
the same table:

1. **The weight/activation relative-error ratio does not predict
   downstream damage monotonically.** Extending `docs/MATH.md` §3's
   2-model table to Qwen: OPT 5.02x ratio → +3.9% PPL damage, Pythia 5.74x
   → +106.2%, **Qwen 2.33x → +100.4%**. Qwen has the *lowest* error ratio
   of the three but the *second-highest* downstream damage — the opposite
   of what the existing fused-QKV-concentration hypothesis (proposed for
   Pythia) would predict if error ratio alone drove the outcome. Flagged
   as a new open question in the draft's Discussion and Future Work,
   alongside a new candidate hypothesis generated while writing this up:
   Pythia and Qwen are both pre-norm and OPT is not (already a known
   architectural oddity, see `docs/PLAN.md`) — noted as exactly as
   unconfirmed as the fused-QKV hypothesis, not elevated above it.
2. **Qwen's control and weights-only results carry unusually high seed
   variance**, most consequentially weights-only's seed=1337 (PPL 28.91)
   against its siblings (18.69, 18.23) — a discrepancy first flagged Day 7
   and never resolved. Reported this directly in the draft's Results
   section with the per-seed breakdown shown alongside the mean±std,
   rather than letting a "+42.3% vs. control" headline number stand
   without the caveat that one outlier seed, not a uniform effect, is
   driving most of it.
