## 2. Important correction, made explicitly rather than left standing

While reporting progress, an early read of the clip-rate data was wrong
and is worth recording as wrong, not quietly fixed. The first 10-minute
window showed 16 clips across 16 steps; the next few windows showed 6-7
clips each. That was reported in chat as "clipping frequency is
declining" -- but each window only *covers* about 6-7 steps, so 6-7
clips per 6-7-step window is **100% engagement**, identical to the first
window, not a decline. Checked directly and precisely once the user
asked "so all of them are clipping?": **114 of 114 steps clipped, exactly
100%**, no tapering at all.

This is a meaningfully different, more precise finding than "clipping
occasionally catches outliers": for this specific configuration
(`activations_only` on Qwen2.5-0.5B, `LEARNING_RATE=1e-5`,
`GRAD_CLIP_NORM=1.0`), the raw pre-clip gradient norm has exceeded the
threshold on *every single step observed so far*. Clipping is functioning
as a continuous, uniform rescaling of every update, not an occasional
safety net for rare outliers.

**Whether 100%-clipped is itself still healthy** was reasoned through
explicitly rather than assumed either way:
- Clipping only rescales the gradient's *magnitude* (direction is
  preserved), so the optimizer still moves in the correct direction on
  every step regardless of whether that step was clipped -- there's no
  mechanism by which 100% clipping alone could corrupt training the way
  the original unclipped explosion did.
- The concrete evidence for whether it's *actually* working is the
  outcome, not the clip rate: loss staying bounded and spike-free through
  115 steps where the original run had already diverged 3 separate times.
  That's the real signal, and it's positive.
- The one legitimate open question this raises: does training at a
  gradient norm uniformly capped to 1.0 converge to as good a result as
  an unclipped-but-lucky run would have, or does the constant capping
  leave some performance on the table? Not resolvable until this run's
  final PPL is in and compared against seed=42's original (unclipped,
  spiked, PPL=94.75) result and, more usefully, against
  `weights_only`/`none`'s control numbers.
